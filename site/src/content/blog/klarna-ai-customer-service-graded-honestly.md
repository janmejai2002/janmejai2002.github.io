## Executive TL;DR

- Klarna's AI assistant handled [2.3 million conversations in its first month](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/), roughly [two-thirds of all Klarna customer service chats](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/) — self-reported, unaudited, one month of data.
- Klarna said the bot did [the equivalent work of 700 full-time agents](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/), a headline number nobody outside the company has verified.
- Customer satisfaction came in [on par with human agents](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/) — not better. That's Klarna's own phrasing, and it matters.
- Resolution time fell from 11 minutes to [under 2](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/), and repeat inquiries dropped [25%](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/).
- Klarna projected an [estimated $40 million profit improvement for 2024](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/) — a forecast, not a booked, audited result.
- Fourteen months later, CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski said cost had been ["a too predominant evaluation factor," producing "lower quality,"](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/) and Klarna started [rehiring humans through a remote, Uber-style gig model](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/).

## The Press Release That Ate the Internet

In February 2024, Klarna put out a press release, and it went everywhere. Not because it was well-written — press releases rarely are — but because the numbers were the kind that get screenshotted into a hundred LinkedIn posts before lunch. An AI assistant, [handling 2.3 million conversations in a single month](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/), doing [the work of 700 people](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/). It sounded like the future had arrived early and on budget.

The details, to be fair, were specific. The assistant ran across [23 markets, 24/7, in more than 35 languages](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/), a scale most customer service teams never touch. It cut resolution time from [11 minutes down to under 2](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/), and repeat inquiries — the annoying kind where a customer has to come back and ask the same question twice — [fell by 25%](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/). The system was [powered by OpenAI](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/), which gave the story an extra layer of relevance at a moment when everyone wanted proof that generative AI could do real, revenue-adjacent work, not just write poems about cats.

And then there was the money. Klarna said the assistant was [estimated to drive $40 million in profit improvement for 2024](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/). Forty million dollars is a real number, the kind that gets a CFO's attention. It's also, notably, a projection for a fiscal year that had barely started, based on one month of live data.

## Read the Fine Print, Not the Headline

Here's the thing about Klarna's own release: it's more careful than most of the coverage that followed it. The company didn't say the AI beat human agents on customer satisfaction. It said satisfaction was [on par with human agents](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/). Equal, not better. That's a meaningfully smaller claim than the one that ended up in a thousand secondhand summaries, and it's worth sitting with, because a lot of what got repeated about this case study simply isn't what Klarna said.

Same with the $40 million. It was never presented as a confirmed figure — it was [estimated](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/), a forward-looking number from a company with every incentive to sound impressive to investors ahead of an IPO push. None of that makes the number fake. It makes it a projection, self-reported by the company that benefits from it sounding big, covering a single month of operation. That's a different category of fact than "audited annual savings," and treating it as the latter is where a lot of the AI-replaces-service-teams narrative went wrong.

## Fourteen Months Later, the Story Changes

Fast forward to May 2025. Bloomberg got Siemiatkowski on the record, and Customer Experience Dive reported the quote that undercut the whole triumphant arc: cost, he said, had been ["a too predominant evaluation factor when organizing this,"](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/) and the result was, in his own word, ["lower quality."](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/)

That's not a subtle walk-back. That's a CEO saying, in public, that the thing his company had used to headline its AI credentials had a quality problem baked into how it was built — optimized too hard for cost, at the expense of what customers actually experienced. Klarna responded by [rehiring human agents through a remote, Uber-style gig platform](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/), a flexible, on-demand staffing model rather than a return to a traditional call center.

## Not a Retreat — a Correction

It would be tidy, and wrong, to call this a full reversal. It wasn't Klarna ripping out the AI and going back to 2019. The company reframed it as a [hybrid, dual-track model](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/), and by the reporting's own account, AI still handles the majority of volume. What changed is the acknowledgment that "majority of volume" and "good enough quality for every case" aren't the same claim, and that Klarna had let them blur together for over a year while the press release did its work.

That's the honest read: a company that leaned hard into an automation number because the number was genuinely good and genuinely useful for a narrative it wanted to tell, then found out a year-plus later that going further wasn't free. Cost-optimizing the evaluation criteria produced a system that was fine at scale and disappointing at the edges — the messy, human, non-average cases that don't show up cleanly in a first-month satisfaction score.

## What To Ask Before You Cite a Vendor's Own Numbers

This case gets cited constantly — in decks, in blog posts, in "AI is already replacing jobs" arguments. Most of the people citing it never open the source. If you're one of the people who does, here's the checklist this case study earns:

- **Is it audited, or self-reported?** Every figure in the original release — 2.3 million conversations, 700 agents, $40 million — came from Klarna, about Klarna, published by Klarna. There's no third-party auditor in that chain.
- **Is it a launch-month number or a sustained one?** One month tells you a system works well enough to launch. It doesn't tell you what it looks like at month fourteen, which is exactly the gap that produced the 2025 correction.
- **What's the actual comparison?** "On par with human agents" is not "better than human agents." The distinction was in the release the whole time; it just got lost in translation.
- **Is the money a result or a forecast?** "$40 million profit improvement" was estimated for a year that had barely begun. Treat projections as projections.
- **What was optimized for?** Siemiatkowski's own answer, over a year later, was cost — and he said that emphasis cost the company quality. If a launch metric is about efficiency, ask what it isn't measuring.

Klarna's numbers weren't lies. They were a company's best first-month read on its own system, reported honestly within their limits, then flattened by everyone downstream who wanted a clean AI-wins-or-AI-fails story. The real story was never that clean. It rarely is.
