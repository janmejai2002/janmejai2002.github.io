## The 1 percent paradox: why massive AI budgets outpace measurable returns

Boardrooms across global financial services are writing double-digit technology checks on faith. Released on November 19, 2026, KPMG's 12th annual [Global Insurance CEO Outlook](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/value-creation/global-ceo-outlook-survey/insurance.html) surveyed 100 insurance CEOs across 12 markets leading carriers, reinsurers, and brokers with annual revenues above USD 500 million. The gap between capital commitment and realized profit is stark: 82 percent of CEOs call AI a top investment priority over the next three years, and 64 percent plan to direct 11 to 20 percent of total budgets toward AI over the coming 12 months, yet only 1 percent report achieving a measurable return on that spending to date ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/insurance-ceos-forecast-stronger-earnings-as-ai-investment-returns-remain-limited-kpmg-reports/)).

Executive optimism has not cooled despite the thin payback. Overall, 87 percent of insurance CEOs express confidence in their own company's three-year growth prospects, and 89 percent of respondents in KPMG's [highlights](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/value-creation/global-ceo-outlook-survey/insurance.html) (and 84 percent expecting a one-to-three-year window in [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/insurance-ceos-forecast-stronger-earnings-as-ai-investment-returns-remain-limited-kpmg-reports/), up from 67 percent in 2025) expect AI investments to pay back within three years. At the same time, 30 percent now project annual earnings growth between 5 and 9.99 percent, double the 15 percent share who held that forecast in 2025 ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/insurance-ceos-forecast-stronger-earnings-as-ai-investment-returns-remain-limited-kpmg-reports/)).

As Frank Pfaffenzeller, Global Head of Insurance at KPMG International, noted in the release, the central task is turning investment and confidence into sustained financial performance ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/insurance-ceos-forecast-stronger-earnings-as-ai-investment-returns-remain-limited-kpmg-reports/)). For CFOs, a 64 percent commitment of 11 to 20 percent of enterprise budgets paired with a 1 percent hit rate signals a structural execution bottleneck, not a minor timing lag.

## The 3 percent modernization trap: why isolated pilots fail to compound

The sharpest diagnostic clue in KPMG's data sits in the gap between 77 percent and 3 percent. Three-quarters of insurance CEOs (77 percent) report significant progress modernizing their core operating models, yet just 3 percent describe their organizations as fully modernized across most essential business functions ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/insurance-ai-spend-soars-as-measurable-returns-stay-out-of-reach-598232.aspx)).

That distinction explains why local AI deployments fail to move the income statement. A 2026 Deloitte Canada analysis cited by [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/insurance-ai-spend-soars-as-measurable-returns-stay-out-of-reach-598232.aspx) calls this an "orchestration gap": insurers test and deploy isolated AI tools quickly, yet value from individual use cases fails to compound across the enterprise because underlying policy administration, billing, and claims systems remain fragmented.

Consider how P&L attribution works inside a property and casualty carrier. Suppose a regional claims team deploys a document-intake model that summarizes medical invoices in 15 seconds instead of 10 minutes. On paper, that looks like a productivity win. In practice, if the claims adjuster still has to copy those fields manually into a 20-year-old mainframe policy system, wait for a separate fraud-scoring batch job overnight, and route physical reserve approvals through email, total claim cycle time and loss-adjustment expenses barely budge. Until workflow steps connect end to end through clean APIs, isolated time savings evaporate into slack capacity and never show up in the combined ratio.

Insurers know where the commercial value sits: 50 percent of surveyed CEOs point to claims transformation and automation as a top-three value driver over the next three years, 49 percent cite underwriting and pricing optimization, and 47 percent highlight policyholder experience and retention ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/insurance-ai-spend-soars-as-measurable-returns-stay-out-of-reach-598232.aspx)). As Matthew Smith, Global Lead of Insurance Strategy and Transformation at KPMG International, put it, the next phase requires moving from incremental improvements toward connected operations that deliver value at scale ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/insurance-ceos-forecast-stronger-earnings-as-ai-investment-returns-remain-limited-kpmg-reports/)).

## Rising implementation bills: unpacking the jump from 37 to 48 percent cost friction

If isolated pilots are cheap to demo, why are enterprise AI bills climbing fast enough to alarm nearly half of CEOs? KPMG reports that the share of insurance executives citing implementation expenses as an obstacle jumped 11 percentage points in a single year, from 37 percent in 2025 to 48 percent in 2026 ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/insurance-ceos-forecast-stronger-earnings-as-ai-investment-returns-remain-limited-kpmg-reports/)).

Three structural costs drive that jump once an AI initiative leaves a sandbox:

- **Legacy data plumbing and integration:** Connecting modern foundation models to fragmented policy and claims databases requires custom middleware, schema mapping, and historical data cleansing that often cost multiples of the underlying model API fees.
- **Regulatory governance and auditability:** In KPMG's survey, 41 percent of insurance CEOs flagged regulatory pressure as a top growth threat, well above the 26 percent cross-industry average ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/insurance-ai-spend-soars-as-measurable-returns-stay-out-of-reach-598232.aspx)). In the United States, more than 25 states and the District of Columbia have adopted the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) AI Model Bulletin, and 12 state departments are piloting a formal AI Risk Evaluation Supplement alongside state-specific rules in Colorado and New York ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/insurance-ai-spend-soars-as-measurable-returns-stay-out-of-reach-598232.aspx)). Every automated underwriting or claims model now requires documented bias testing, explainability logs, and human-review trails.
- **Competing for scarce technical talent:** While 83 percent of insurers now include AI hiring in long-term workforce plans (up from 54 percent in 2025) and 81 percent are redesigning roles around AI collaboration (up from 66 percent), 52 percent still cite skills gaps as a major constraint, forcing carriers to rely on expensive external integrators or targeted M&A ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/insurance-ceos-forecast-stronger-earnings-as-ai-investment-returns-remain-limited-kpmg-reports/); [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/insurance-ai-spend-soars-as-measurable-returns-stay-out-of-reach-598232.aspx)).

## The CFO capital gate: tying 11 to 20 percent AI budgets to core P&L milestones

To avoid funding another year of unmeasured pilots while implementation bills climb, CFOs and transformation leads should gate 2027 AI capital behind four concrete rules:

1. **Require API and data readiness before releasing model spend.** Hold back application-layer AI funding in any line of business whose core claims or underwriting system still lacks real-time read-write APIs and unified customer identifiers.
1. **Fund end-to-end workflows, never standalone point tools.** Approve business cases only when every handoff from intake to decisioning and reserve posting is mapped, ensuring saved minutes translate into shorter claim cycle times or higher underwriter quote-to-bind capacity.
1. **Price compliance and integration into the initial business case.** Mandate that every AI proposal include line-item budgets for data engineering, NAIC-style governance documentation, and post-launch monitoring alongside model inference costs.
1. **Release budgets in 90-day P&L tranches.** Split annual allocations into staged gates tied to audited unit-economic metrics, such as loss-adjustment expense per claim or underwriting turnaround time, pausing expansion if baseline metrics do not move.

Before signing off on next year's 11 to 20 percent budget tranche, board audit committees should ask five blunt questions of any department claiming "significant modernization progress":

- Can our claims or underwriting AI read from and write back to the core system of record without manual copy-paste?
- Which specific P&L line (combined ratio, expense ratio, or retention rate) will move within 12 months, and who owns that target?
- What percentage of the proposed AI budget goes to data plumbing and regulatory audit controls versus vendor licenses?
- Have we retired any legacy manual step, or simply added an AI summary layer on top of existing headcount workflows?
- If measurable financial returns stay at 1 percent after two quarters, what automatic kill-switch reallocates that capital?
