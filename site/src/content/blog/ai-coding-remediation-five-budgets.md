## Executive TL;DR

- **93% higher code output triggers a 94% surge in security cleanup:** According to *The AI Remediation Bill* (an ActiveState / Moving Horizon study of **307 enterprise leaders** at `1,000+` employee organizations featured on [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)), **93%** report higher engineering output and **91%** release more often, yet **94%** report a surge in code requiring remediation and **97%** face heavier governance workloads.
- **Engineers lose 139 hours per year (17+ working days) fixing AI-assisted code:** Across surveyed enterprises, **91% of engineers spend at least 5% of their time revisiting AI-assisted work**, averaging a conservative floor of **6.7% of capacity (`139 hours` per engineer annually, or `13,900+ hours` per 100 engineers, equal to ~7 full-time hires)** ([PR Newswire / ActiveState](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activestate-research-finds-ai-assisted-development-could-consume-13-900-engineering-hours-annually-at-enterprises-302911624.html)).
- **The hidden remediation bill splits across five separate departmental budgets:** Beyond Engineering rework, unmanaged AI-introduced dependencies delayed software releases at **76% of Operations teams**, triggered audit findings at **58% of Compliance teams**, caused confirmed security incidents at **32% of Security teams**, and drove rising risk costs for **83% of Finance/leadership teams** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)).
- **Only 31% of enterprises have ever totaled the five-budget bill:** Because rework, release delays, and audit findings sit in separate cost centers, **69% of enterprises have never formally quantified the full cost** (`47%` rely on informal guesses) ([PR Newswire / ActiveState](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activestate-research-finds-ai-assisted-development-could-consume-13-900-engineering-hours-annually-at-enterprises-302911624.html)).
- **Paper policies fail at machine speed (`92%` vs `52%`):** While **92%** of enterprises have a formal open-source policy, only **52% follow it consistently**, and **50% admit new dependencies reach production with zero prior review** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)).

## The Velocity Mirage: Why 93 Percent Higher Output Triggers 94 Percent More Cleanup

When software vendors pitch AI coding assistants to the board, the return-on-investment slide is seductively simple: multiply developer headcount by a 30% increase in pull-request merge velocity. What that slide leaves out is what happens to your vulnerability scanner queue, your release calendar, and your audit team two weeks after those AI-generated pull requests merge.

On November 16, 2026, *The Hacker News* published findings from **The AI Remediation Bill**, a quantitative study commissioned by ActiveState and conducted by Moving Horizon across **307 security, governance, platform, and engineering leaders** (plus 10 in-depth executive interviews) at North American and Western European enterprises with **1,000 or more employees and 100+ engineers** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activestate-research-finds-ai-assisted-development-could-consume-13-900-engineering-hours-annually-at-enterprises-302911624.html)).

The data confirms that AI coding tools genuinely accelerate top-of-funnel code writing, while simultaneously creating a downstream remediation wave ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activestate-research-finds-ai-assisted-development-could-consume-13-900-engineering-hours-annually-at-enterprises-302911624.html)):

- **The Productivity Gain Is Real:** **93% of enterprises report higher engineering output**, and **91% ship software releases more frequently**.
- **The Cleanup Surge Matches It 1-for-1:** **94% report that AI has increased the volume of software requiring security remediation or cleanup**, **94% say AI has accelerated open-source package consumption**, and **97% report that their software governance workload has increased**.
- **Governance Is Falling Behind:** **83% of enterprise leaders admit that developer output is outpacing their organization's governance capability**, even as **71%** optimistically assume their legacy review processes will somehow scale ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)).

## One Bill, Five Budgets: Tracing the Hidden Costs Across Engineering, Ops, and Compliance

Why have CFOs and CTOs failed to spot a remediation wave that hits `94%` of large enterprises? Because **the productivity gain lands in one metric (Engineering commits), while the cleanup bill is split across five separate departmental budgets with no single owner** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)).

Start with Engineering's own hidden tax. In the survey, **91% of engineering organizations report that developers spend at least 5% of their working hours fixing or revisiting AI-assisted code** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)). At the study's conservative floor, that equals **6.7% of total engineering time, or 139 hours per engineer per year (more than 17 full working days)** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activestate-research-finds-ai-assisted-development-could-consume-13-900-engineering-hours-annually-at-enterprises-302911624.html)). In a division with **100 engineers**, AI code rework consumes **at least 13,900 engineering hours every year**, equivalent to the full annual capacity of **roughly seven full-time engineers** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activestate-research-finds-ai-assisted-development-could-consume-13-900-engineering-hours-annually-at-enterprises-302911624.html)).

And those `139 hours` per developer are only the first fifth of the invoice. Here is how *The AI Remediation Bill* breaks down across all five enterprise cost centers ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activestate-research-finds-ai-assisted-development-could-consume-13-900-engineering-hours-annually-at-enterprises-302911624.html); [GLOG.AI](https://www.glog.ai/ai-remediation-bill-roi-calculator/)):

| **1. Engineering** | **91%** spend `>= 5%` of time fixing AI-assisted code | **6.7% of capacity (`139 hrs/engineer/yr`)**, or **13,900+ hrs/yr** per 100 engineers (~7 FTEs) | Logged as routine bug-fixing or sprint spillover rather than AI rework |

Operations / DevOps** | **76%** had a release delayed by unmanaged open-source dependencies in the past year | **36% suffered multiple delayed releases** in 12 months (~11% of releases impacted per [GLOG.AI](https://www.glog.ai/ai-remediation-bill-roi-calculator/)) | Tracked as deployment or CI/CD pipeline downtime, disconnected from AI coding seats |

As ActiveState Field Engineering Manager Leslie Pascual observed in *The Hacker News*, in quarterly governance reviews the CISO brings an incident count and the VP of Platform brings a list of delayed releases, yet neither executive realizes both numbers trace back to the exact same unvetted packages entering the repository through AI autocomplete ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)).

## The Dependency Explosion: Why Human-Speed Policies Fail Machine-Speed Imports

Why does AI-assisted coding hit open-source governance and Operations release schedules so hard? Because **AI coding assistants resolve problems by importing third-party libraries at machine speed**.

When developers wrote every `import` statement by hand, a loosely enforced open-source policy leaked slowly. Today, an AI coding agent can suggest a package, pull in its direct dependencies, and cascade dozens of transitive dependencies into a build in three seconds. In the survey, **96% of leaders say AI has increased dependency growth** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)).

That machine-speed import loop has shattered traditional "policy-and-scanner" governance ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activestate-research-finds-ai-assisted-development-could-consume-13-900-engineering-hours-annually-at-enterprises-302911624.html)):

- **The 92%-to-52% Policy Collapse:** **92% of surveyed enterprises have a formal open-source policy on paper, but only 52% say it is consistently followed.** A policy that relies on a human engineer pausing to check a license and CVE database before accepting an AI completion cannot survive a 5x jump in code volume.
- **Half of Dependencies Bypass Pre-Production Review:** **50% of enterprises admit that a brand-new open-source dependency can reach production without any prior review or approval** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)).
- **Scanners Only Record What Already Got In:** **64% of enterprises say most of their governance activity happens *after* an alert fires**, and **63% address open-source vulnerabilities only after a post-build scanner flags them** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)). As IDC Program VP Jim Mercer noted in the report, static scanning after the fact is permanently a step behind when an assistant can write and push package-calling code toward production in minutes ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/activestate-research-finds-ai-assisted-development-could-consume-13-900-engineering-hours-annually-at-enterprises-302911624.html)).
- **The Prevention Deficit (`26%` vs `37%`):** Across all respondents, teams currently spend only **26% of their governance effort on pre-deployment prevention** versus 74% on post-alert cleanup, stating that prevention should be at least **37%**. Among organizations experiencing the heaviest strain, prevention sits at just **19% against a 41% target** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)).

## Pricing Net Velocity: Pre-Commit Gates and Five-Budget ROI Accounting

For **55% of surveyed executives, control (stopping unapproved packages from entering the codebase at all) is now a bigger challenge than visibility (`45%`)** ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)). Before renewing or expanding AI coding seats for 2027, CTOs, CISOs, and CFOs should execute this five-step remediation playbook ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html); [GLOG.AI](https://www.glog.ai/ai-remediation-bill-roi-calculator/)):

1. **Put One Consolidated Number on the Five-Budget Bill This Quarter:** Join the **31%** of enterprises that audit net velocity rather than raw commits. Calculate `net_ai_coding_roi = gross_dev_hours_saved - (eng_rework_hours + appsec_triage_hours + ops_release_delay_cost + compliance_audit_hours)`. Using the study's `139 hours/engineer/year` rework floor (`13,900 hours` per 100 developers) plus `0.333 hours` per AppSec alert immediately grounds vendor seat negotiations in financial reality.
1. **Assign a Single Intake Owner Across All Five Teams:** Right now, five budgets pay for bad dependencies, but nobody owns the front door. Designate one platform/security owner with the authority to define which open-source packages and versions are eligible to enter the build environment ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)).
1. **Restrict AI Coding Assistants to a Curated Package Allowlist:** Close the `92%`-policy-vs-`52%`-compliance gap by configuring IDE coding agents (`Cursor`, `Claude Code`, `GitHub Copilot`, `Codex`) and package managers (`pip`, `npm`, `Maven`) to pull **only from an internal, pre-vetted artifact repository** rather than the public internet.
1. **Mandate SLSA Provenance and Automated SBOM/CBOM Generation:** Require Supply-chain Levels for Software Artifacts (**SLSA**) build attestations for third-party components and automate Software/Cryptography Bills of Materials (**SBOM/CBOM**) in CI, which cuts compliance audit preparation labor by up to **78%** under EU Cyber Resilience Act, NIS2, and DORA rules ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html); [GLOG.AI](https://www.glog.ai/ai-remediation-bill-roi-calculator/)).
1. **Make Post-Build Scanners Your Verification Check, Not Your First Filter:** Shift governance budget from the `19% to 26%` prevention baseline up to the `37% to 41%` target. Every hour spent blocking bad packages at intake eliminates recurring charges across Engineering, AppSec, Operations, Compliance, and Finance simultaneously ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/expert-insights/2026/11/ai-made-your-developers-faster-five.html)).
