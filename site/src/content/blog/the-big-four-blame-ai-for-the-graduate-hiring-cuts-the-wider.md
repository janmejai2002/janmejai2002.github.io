## Executive TL;DR

- KPMG cut its graduate recruitment scheme 29%, from 1,399 hires in 2023 to 942 last year ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/big-four-slash-graduate-jobs-as-ai-takes-over-entry-level-tasks)).
- Deloitte trimmed graduate intake 18%, from 1,700 to 1,400, and is planning flat recruitment ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/big-four-slash-graduate-jobs-as-ai-takes-over-entry-level-tasks)).
- Indeed shows UK graduate job postings down 12% year-on-year and Adzuna shows a 32% drop over three years, but the Institute of Student Employers, surveying 69 large employers, found vacancies down a milder 7%, and Target Connect logged postings up 8% ([Varsity](https://www.varsity.co.uk/features/30269)).
- PwC still hired 1,300 graduates in the UK and 3,200 in the US last year, even as it cut more than 5,600 roles worldwide and scrapped a 2021 plan to add 100,000 staff over five years ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cm272510e6jo)).

## KPMG Cut Recruitment 29%. Deloitte Cut It 18%.

Start with the numbers the Big Four don't dispute. KPMG's graduate recruitment scheme fell from 1,399 hires in 2023 to 942 last year, a 29% cut, with roughly 1,000 expected this year ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/big-four-slash-graduate-jobs-as-ai-takes-over-entry-level-tasks)). Deloitte moved from 1,700 graduate hires to 1,400, an 18% reduction, and is planning flat recruitment for this year ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/big-four-slash-graduate-jobs-as-ai-takes-over-entry-level-tasks)). Between them, the Big Four employ around 100,000 people in the UK, and over the past two years they've cut hundreds of roles, concentrated at graduate and school-leaver level ([Public Practice Recruitment Ltd on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/big-four-replacing-graduates-ai-public-practice-recruitment-ltd-dclkc)). One LinkedIn post captured the whole pattern in a single headline number: AI, the post noted, was already being blamed in the press for a 30% drop in graduate roles at the Big Four before the post was even written ([Jo Whight on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jo-whight_ai-was-getting-the-blame-in-the-headlines-activity-7345300190897090560-dPeE)).

But the same post also included a number that complicates that blame.

## Adoption Isn't Impact

Data shared by Jon Hering, cited in the same post: 80% of companies are using AI, yet only 20% report meaningful impact from it ([Jo Whight on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jo-whight_ai-was-getting-the-blame-in-the-headlines-activity-7345300190897090560-dPeE)). Financial services tells a near-identical story. Progress Together's RISE with AI report found 83% of FS firms piloting generative AI, but only 8% had scaled it effectively ([Jo Whight on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jo-whight_ai-was-getting-the-blame-in-the-headlines-activity-7345300190897090560-dPeE), citing the report). So the industry blamed for firing graduates hasn't shown it can run AI at the scale that would explain a decision this size.

Ambitious forecasts don't settle the question either. Cem Dilmegani, who spent a decade as a McKinsey consultant, argues on AIMultiple that 90% of the white-collar roles he's seen "can be automated today with the right agent harness" ([AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/ai-job-loss)). Elsewhere on the same page, other AI experts are cited predicting that half of entry-level white-collar jobs will be lost by 2030 ([AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/ai-job-loss)). That's a forecast for five years out. It is not a count of what's already happened at KPMG this year.

## Three Job Boards, Three Different Numbers

The graduate market beyond the Big Four doesn't move in step with any of this. Indeed's UK data shows the share of graduate jobs at its lowest level since at least 2018, with postings down 12% on a year earlier; Adzuna shows a steeper 32% drop over three years ([Varsity](https://www.varsity.co.uk/features/30269)). Survey the Institute of Student Employers' 69 largest employers, though, and the fall is milder: vacancies down 7% ([Varsity](https://www.varsity.co.uk/features/30269)). Check Target Connect, the platform run by Group GTI, and postings were actually up 8% in the 2024/5 hiring season compared with the year before ([Varsity](https://www.varsity.co.uk/features/30269)). Three measures. Three verdicts. Same graduate market.

## PwC Still Hired Thousands

Look at what a single firm's own numbers do to the tidy story. PwC hired 1,300 graduates in the UK and 3,200 in the US last year ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cm272510e6jo)), while also cutting more than 5,600 roles worldwide and quietly dropping a 2021 plan to add 100,000 staff over five years ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cm272510e6jo)). Those aren't the actions of a firm that simply swapped graduate labor for a chatbot. Scrapping a five-year hiring target is what a company does when the plan itself stopped making sense, regardless of what AI can or can't do.

## Why AI Is the Easiest Line in the Press Release

CNBC's reporting on AI-linked layoffs names the pattern bluntly: firms are blaming AI for job cuts, and critics say it's "a good excuse" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/19/firms-are-blaming-ai-for-job-cuts-critics-say-its-a-good-excuse.html)). Lufthansa says it will cut 4,000 jobs by 2030 while leaning on AI for efficiency ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/19/firms-are-blaming-ai-for-job-cuts-critics-say-its-a-good-excuse.html)). Salesforce laid off 4,000 customer-support staff in September, saying AI now does half the work in that unit ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/19/firms-are-blaming-ai-for-job-cuts-critics-say-its-a-good-excuse.html)). Klarna has reduced headcount by 40% while pushing AI adoption hard ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/19/firms-are-blaming-ai-for-job-cuts-critics-say-its-a-good-excuse.html)). None of these figures are about graduate hiring. They show a pattern instead: when a company needs a story for a cut, AI is the line that makes it sound strategic instead of overdue.

## The Ladder Gets Pulled Up Unevenly

There's a stat in Whight's post worth sitting with longer than the rest. Only 38% of employees from lower socio-economic backgrounds have access to AI at work, against 48% from higher socio-economic backgrounds ([Jo Whight on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jo-whight_ai-was-getting-the-blame-in-the-headlines-activity-7345300190897090560-dPeE), citing Progress Together's RISE with AI report). If entry-level roles keep shrinking while AI access stays this uneven, the graduates most likely to lose the bottom rung are the ones least likely to have had a hand on the tool blamed for removing it.

Ask a Big Four partner why the graduate scheme shrank, and AI is the answer that fits on a slide. Ask Indeed, ISE, and Target Connect the same question, and you get three different numbers and no consensus. The one figure everyone agrees on: nobody's hiring 100,000 people over five years anymore.

### LinkedIn Post

KPMG and Deloitte both cite AI for double-digit cuts to graduate hiring — but the data tells a messier story. Worth a closer look before accepting the easy explanation.
