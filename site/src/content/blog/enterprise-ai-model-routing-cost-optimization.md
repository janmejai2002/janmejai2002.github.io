## The 69 percent concentration: why frontier labs still own most enterprise AI dollars

Enterprise AI budgets expanded far faster than corporate procurement teams anticipated in 2026. According to Menlo Ventures' November 3, 2026 [State of Enterprise AI](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/03/3394279/0/en/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-report-enterprise-ai-spending-hits-177b-up-5-4x-in-one-year-as-agents-take-over.html) report, surveying nearly 400 U.S. enterprise decision-makers overseeing over USD 4 billion in AI spending, total enterprise AI deployment spend hit an annualized USD 176.8 billion by September 2026, up 5.4x from USD 33 billion in 2025. Yet the share of enterprises paying for AI rose modestly from 55 percent to 65 percent, meaning existing buyers are driving the surge by spending 2.5x what they budgeted.

Instead of fragmenting across dozens of model vendors, capital concentrated at the top. Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google together capture 69 percent of enterprise AI deployment spending, up from 50 percent a year earlier ([Menlo Ventures](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/03/3394279/0/en/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-report-enterprise-ai-spending-hits-177b-up-5-4x-in-one-year-as-agents-take-over.html)). Within enterprise LLM API spending specifically, Menlo Ventures reports Anthropic climbed from 40 percent to 55 percent, OpenAI rose from 27 percent to 31 percent, and Google shifted from 21 percent to 9 percent (note that Menlo Ventures discloses it is an investor in both Anthropic and OpenRouter) ([explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-177-billion-anthropic-55-percent-google-9-percent-api-share-2026)).

For procurement and finance teams, Menlo Ventures's dataset also underscores that AI is expanding software budgets rather than cannibalizing core systems of record: 55 percent of incremental AI spend came from net-new budgets, while 22 percent came from existing software budgets as incumbents converted seats to AI tiers ([Menlo Ventures](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/03/3394279/0/en/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-report-enterprise-ai-spending-hits-177b-up-5-4x-in-one-year-as-agents-take-over.html)). At the same time, [explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-177-billion-anthropic-55-percent-google-9-percent-api-share-2026) notes that enterprise buyers should avoid picking vendors from a single share chart because API spend shares shift rapidly with every major model release and exclude direct cloud-platform revenue.

Why do buyers stick with the three frontier labs? Switching friction is high when an enterprise already holds cloud-commit credits with Google, Microsoft Azure (OpenAI), or AWS (Anthropic), and engineering teams default to the highest-reliability model during early agent rollouts because coding alone represents USD 34 billion of the USD 77 billion enterprise application market ([Menlo Ventures](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/03/3394279/0/en/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-report-enterprise-ai-spending-hits-177b-up-5-4x-in-one-year-as-agents-take-over.html)).

## The 80 percent agent tax: why paying frontier rates for routine loops kills margin

Defaulting every API call to a flagship frontier endpoint worked when humans typed a few dozen prompts a day. That assumption breaks once autonomous agents take over. Menlo Ventures reports that on OpenRouter, autonomous agents generate 80 percent of all tokens, roughly seven times human-driven traffic, after growing 37x since February 2026 ([Menlo Ventures](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/03/3394279/0/en/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-report-enterprise-ai-spending-hits-177b-up-5-4x-in-one-year-as-agents-take-over.html)).

Consider what happens inside a production agent loop. A single customer-support or code-review workflow makes dozens of background calls: classifying an incoming ticket, extracting JSON fields from a PDF, summarizing thread history, checking policy rules, and drafting a reply. Only one or two of those steps require frontier reasoning. Paying flagship frontier per-token rates for routine extraction and classification is pure margin leakage.

Notice the telling ratio in Menlo Ventures' own numbers: open-source models already process roughly 1 in 5 tokens (20 percent) while accounting for only 1 in 25 model dollars (4 percent) ([Menlo Ventures](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/03/3394279/0/en/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-report-enterprise-ai-spending-hits-177b-up-5-4x-in-one-year-as-agents-take-over.html)). Sophisticated buyers are already routing high-volume, repetitive agent steps to cheaper models while reserving expensive frontier contracts for complex synthesis.

## Serverless open weights: capturing 30 percent discounts without GPU ops overhead

Historically, capturing open-weight cost savings forced enterprises to lease dedicated GPU clusters, manage vLLM autoscaling, and absorb idle capacity overnight. Serverless inference endpoints eliminate that operational tax. On November 22, 2026, [Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/together-ai-brings-qwen4-max-to-serverless-inference-at-30-off/) reported that Together AI onboarded Alibaba's 2.4-trillion-parameter Qwen4 Max (71B active parameters, 1M-token context window) onto its serverless inference platform at a 30 percent introductory discount: USD 1.05 per 1 million input tokens, USD 0.0525 per 1 million cached input tokens, and USD 3.15 per 1 million output tokens.

To put those serverless economics in concrete terms, Together AI fed all 344 files of the Tornado Python framework (652,519 tokens) into a single request and received a working bug patch in 72 seconds for USD 0.69 at the promotional rate, or USD 0.98 at list price ([Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/together-ai-brings-qwen4-max-to-serverless-inference-at-30-off/)). When Together repeated a roughly 630,000-token request with automatic prompt caching, first-token latency fell from 54 seconds to 8 seconds and input cost dropped 95 percent, from roughly USD 0.66 to roughly USD 0.03 ([Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/together-ai-brings-qwen4-max-to-serverless-inference-at-30-off/)).

| Inference model | Pricing structure | Operational overhead | Best-fit enterprise workload |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Frontier Lab APIs** (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) | Premium per-token + enterprise commits | Zero GPU ops; strict vendor lock-in risk | Complex multi-step reasoning, architecture, high-stakes code ([Menlo Ventures](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/03/3394279/0/en/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-report-enterprise-ai-spending-hits-177b-up-5-4x-in-one-year-as-agents-take-over.html)) |
| **Serverless Open/Alt APIs** (e.g., Together AI) | Pay-per-token (30% discounts + 95% cache savings) | Zero GPU ops; easy multi-model switching | Routine classification, extraction, summarization, long-context repos ([Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/together-ai-brings-qwen4-max-to-serverless-inference-at-30-off/)) |
| **Self-Hosted GPU Clusters** | Fixed hourly/reserved GPU capex | High (DevOps, autoscaling, idle utilization) | Strict data residency or steady 24/7 high-utilization inference |

Consider a straightforward unit-margin calculation for an enterprise processing 10 billion monthly tokens at a blended frontier rate of USD 3.00 per million tokens (USD 30,000 per month, or USD 360,000 per year). If engineering routes 60 percent of those routine agent calls (6 billion tokens) to a serverless tier priced 30 percent lower (USD 2.10 per million tokens), monthly spend drops by USD 5,400, saving USD 64,800 per year before even counting prompt-cache discounts.

## The dual-tier contract playbook: routing workloads and negotiating flexible commits

To capture that margin without degrading application quality, CTOs and procurement leads should implement a four-step workload triage flow alongside tighter 2027 contract terms:

1. **Audit your agent call graph by task complexity.** Log every LLM request for 14 days and tag each call as Tier-1 (complex synthesis, multi-file code edits, edge-case customer escalations) or Tier-2 (classification, JSON extraction, routing, summarization).
1. **Benchmark Tier-2 tasks on serverless endpoints.** Run your real evaluation dataset on two serverless models and verify accuracy, latency, and formatting before migrating traffic off frontier endpoints ([explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-177-billion-anthropic-55-percent-google-9-percent-api-share-2026)).
1. **Route through a unified model gateway.** Place an OpenAI-compatible gateway or router in front of your applications so teams can shift traffic percentages between frontier and serverless providers in minutes whenever a new model shifts price-performance ([explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/menlo-ventures-2026-state-of-enterprise-ai-177-billion-anthropic-55-percent-google-9-percent-api-share-2026)).
1. **Enable automatic prompt caching on shared repo and policy contexts.** Ensure long system prompts and codebase contexts hit cached input tiers, cutting repeat-turn token bills by up to 95 percent ([Futurum Group](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/together-ai-brings-qwen4-max-to-serverless-inference-at-30-off/)).

Before signing 2027 enterprise agreements, procurement teams should run this three-point contract flexibility checklist:

- **Cap annual minimum commitments at 50 percent of projected usage:** Avoid locking 100 percent of forecast agent spend into a single frontier lab so you are never penalized for routing Tier-2 traffic to cheaper serverless APIs.
- **Verify cloud-marketplace drawdown portability:** Confirm whether hyperscaler commit credits can fund third-party serverless endpoints as well as first-party frontier models.
- **Include quarterly rate-card reset clauses:** Because inference prices fall rapidly across releases, tie multi-year volume discounts to current public list prices or a lower-bound price-protection clause.
