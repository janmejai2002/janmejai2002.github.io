# Canon: Calm Technology & Ambient Computing

## 1. Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown: The Coming Age of Calm Technology (1995/1996)
*Xerox Palo Alto Research Center (PARC)*

### The Fundamental Paradigm: Focal vs. Peripheral Attention
Weiser and Brown established the foundational principle of calm computing: **A technology is calm when it moves effortlessly between the periphery of a person\'s attention and its center.**

- **The Center**: Focal attention is demanding, high-energy, and finite. When a computer program constantly demands focal attention (through popups, alert badges, unsolicited chat dialogs, flashing elements), it induces cognitive fatigue, anxiety, and irritation.
- **The Periphery**: Peripheral attention is vast, intuitive, and operates subconsciously. Humans are constantly processing subtle ambient cues—the rustle of wind through an open window, the sound of rain on a roof, the rhythmic hum of an engine, the movement of shadows in a room. We are aware of them without being distracted by them.
- **The Shift**: The hallmark of calm technology is the seamless transition between periphery and center. When something requires attention, it gently comes to the foreground; when satisfied or acknowledged, it recedes gracefully back into the ambient periphery.

### Key Examples & Mechanics
- **The Dangling String (Natalie Jeremijenko)**: An 8-foot piece of plastic string hanging from the ceiling, connected to a small stepper motor wired to the PARC Ethernet cable. When network traffic is quiet, the string hangs motionless or twitches slightly. When heavy traffic surges, the string whirs and flutters furiously. People in the office do not stare at the string; they simply absorb the ambient state of the network through their peripheral vision and hearing.
- **Inner Office Windows**: Glass panes between hallways and offices that allow one to peripherally sense whether a colleague is busy, on the phone, or available, without knocking or interrupting.

### Why Calm Technology Enhances Human Agency
Weiser and Brown argued that calm technology does not diminish agency—it amplifies it. By putting information into the periphery, calm technology *enforms* us (shapes our background awareness) rather than *overburdening* us. It allows people to feel centered and at home in an environment saturated with digital systems.

## 2. Amber Case: Principles of Calm Technology (2015)
*MIT Media Lab / O\'Reilly Media*

Amber Case formalized Weiser\'s vision into eight actionable design principles for modern interactive software:

1. **Technology should require the smallest possible amount of attention**:
   Attention is the scarcest human commodity. Software should be designed for the minimum necessary cognitive footprint.
2. **Technology should inform and create calm**:
   A well-designed ambient interface leaves the user feeling rested, empowered, and clear-headed, never agitated or overstimulated.
3. **Technology should make use of the periphery**:
   Primary content (reading an essay, analyzing an architectural postmortem) remains center-stage. Ambient agents and indicators must reside quietly in negative space and margins.
4. **Technology should amplify the best of technology and the best of humanity**:
   Computers excel at high-speed calculation, continuous state persistence, and geometric simulation; humans excel at narrative synthesis, critical judgment, and aesthetic taste. The interface should never pretend to be human or simulate fake social intimacy.
5. **Technology can communicate, but doesn\'t need to speak**:
   Haptic feedback, subtle hue transitions, breathing physics, pupillary gaze tracking, and physical postures communicate richer and more dignified state than verbose speech bubbles and robotic chat popups.
6. **Technology should consider what happens when it fails**:
   Graceful degradation is paramount. If WebGL fails, the ceramic plate renders as an elegant SVG/CSS plate. If an agent\'s speech fails, it remains dignified in its resting stance.
7. **The right amount of technology is the minimum needed to solve the problem**:
   Eliminate superfluous friction, unnecessary modal interruptions, and performative animation loops.
8. **Technology should respect social and spatial norms**:
   An application should respect the sanctity of a reading environment, treating the reader with the deference of an ancient library or a master craftsman\'s workshop.
